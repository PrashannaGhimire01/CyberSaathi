# CyberSaathi — Evaluation Study Design

## 1. Purpose
This study evaluates whether CyberSaathi helps non-technical users in Nepal
recognise and respond to scam messages, and whether its AI-generated,
plain-language explanations improve understanding. It directly addresses:

- **RQ1** — Do AI-generated explanations improve users' *understanding* of why
  a message is risky?
- **RQ4** — Does the educational content *reduce unsafe behaviour* / improve
  users' ability to identify scams?

RQ2 (detection accuracy) and RQ3 (offline protection) are answered by technical
evaluation (held-out test metrics and functional offline testing) and are
reported separately; this study focuses on the two questions that require human
participants.

## 2. Study type and rationale
A **small-scale, within-subjects, mixed-methods evaluation** (N = 8).

- *Within-subjects* (each participant is their own before/after comparison)
  because it extracts the most signal from a small sample.
- *Mixed-methods*: quantitative measures (accuracy, comprehension scores,
  Likert ratings) plus short qualitative feedback.
- Because N = 8, results are treated as **indicative, not statistically
  conclusive**. This is framed honestly as a pilot evaluation; claims are
  descriptive (direction and size of change, participant quotes), not
  significance tests.

## 3. Participants
- 8 adults (18+), non-technical smartphone users, recruited from family and
  community (a convenience sample — stated openly as a limitation).
- Inclusion: owns/uses a smartphone, can read Nepali and/or English, not an
  IT/security professional.
- Each gives informed consent before taking part and may withdraw at any time.

## 4. Ethics
- Ethics/There approval obtained before any session (per university
  requirements).
- Written informed consent; purpose, voluntary participation, and right to
  withdraw explained in the participant's language.
- **No real personal messages are collected.** All test messages are
  researcher-created. No participant's own SMS, contacts, or accounts are
  accessed.
- Data is anonymised (Participant 1–8, no names); stored on an encrypted,
  password-protected device; used only for this dissertation.
- Because the topic is scams/fraud, participants are debriefed and given a
  short "how to stay safe" summary at the end, so everyone leaves better off.

## 5. Procedure (~25–30 min per participant)
1. **Welcome & consent** (3 min) — explain the study, obtain signed consent.
2. **Pre-test** (6 min) — participant judges 8 messages (mix of scam and
   legit) as "scam" or "safe" and rates confidence (1–5). No app, no feedback.
   → baseline scam-identification ability (RQ4 baseline).
3. **Intervention** (10 min):
   - Participant checks several messages in the app. For half (randomised),
     they see the *full explanation*; for the other half, a *verdict only*
     (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW with no "Why?"). After each, they say *in their own
     words* why it's risky. → RQ1 comparison.
   - Participant works through the Learn lessons. → RQ4 intervention.
4. **Post-test** (6 min) — 8 *new*, difficulty-matched messages judged the same
   way as the pre-test. → change in ability (RQ4).
5. **Questionnaire & feedback** (4 min) — Likert items on clarity, trust,
   usefulness, and the value of Nepali/bilingual explanations, plus 2 open
   questions.
6. **Debrief** — thank, give the safety summary, answer questions.

Message sets are counterbalanced (order varied) to reduce order effects.

## 6. Measures — how each RQ is answered
- **RQ1 (understanding):** for each checked message, the participant's spoken
  reason is scored 0 (no correct reason) / 1 (partial) / 2 (clear correct
  reason). Compare mean comprehension score for *with-explanation* vs
  *verdict-only* messages. Also a self-rated understanding Likert item.
- **RQ4 (behaviour/ability):** compare pre-test vs post-test scam-identification
  accuracy (out of 8) per participant; also change in false "safe" calls on
  scams (the dangerous error) and confidence calibration.
- Supporting: questionnaire ratings (trust, clarity, bilingual value) and
  qualitative themes.

## 7. Analysis plan
- Descriptive statistics: per-participant and mean pre/post accuracy,
  comprehension scores, rating distributions.
- Paired comparison (pre vs post; with- vs without-explanation) reported with
  direction and effect size; an exploratory Wilcoxon signed-rank test may be
  reported but is **underpowered at N = 8** and labelled as such.
- Qualitative feedback analysed by simple thematic grouping.

## 8. Limitations and threats to validity
- Small convenience sample (family/community) → limited generalisability;
  possible social-desirability bias (participants may want to "please" the
  researcher).
- Within-subjects design risks learning/practice effects and fatigue
  (mitigated by matched, counterbalanced sets and a short session).
- Researcher is not blind to conditions → scoring of spoken reasons uses a
  fixed 0/1/2 rubric to reduce bias.
- The test messages are researcher-created and may not capture the full variety
  of real scams.

## 9. Outputs
Per-participant results table, summary statistics, selected quotes, and a short
discussion mapping findings back to RQ1 and RQ4 — ready to drop into the
dissertation's evaluation chapter.
